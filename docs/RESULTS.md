# Results

Produced by the scripts in this repository. Every figure below is regenerated by
running them; nothing here is transcribed by hand from an earlier run.

All models are Tweedie GLMs with a log link and variance power 1.5, or
gradient-boosted Tweedie models, fitted on 102,900 Louisiana claims and scored on
25,726 held out. Headline figures are given for the reference split (seed 42) and
as a mean over 20 repeated splits.

## 1. Reproduction

`scripts/02_reproduce.py`

| Quantity | Value | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Rows / train / test | 128,626 / 102,900 / 25,726 | exact |
| Baseline Gini | 0.0954 | 0.0954 |
| Elevation model Gini | 0.1252 | 0.1252 |
| Gradient boosting Gini | 0.3360 | 0.3356 |
| Portfolio mean loss | $61,213 | $61,213 |
| Credibility k, ad-hoc estimator | 19.748 | 19.7 |
| Max relativity deviation | 0.0005 | under 0.001 |

The baseline is flood zone, occupancy group and structure age at loss. It is a
deliberately non-trivial competitor, not a straw man. The elevation model adds
the ERF through a five-knot hinge spline plus an ERF-by-zone interaction, twelve
parameters in total.

`spec.credibility_uniform_exposure` computes the second.

## 2. Feature set against model class

`scripts/03_factorial.py`

Comparing an elevation GLM against a boosted model on the full terrain feature
set changes two things at once. Crossing them separates the effects.

| | Zone + ERF | Full terrain |
|---|---|---|
| **GLM** | 0.1297 | 0.1478 |
| **Gradient boosting** | 0.1765 | 0.3344 |

| Effect | 20 splits |
|---|---|
| Feature set, model class held fixed | +0.0880 ± 0.0017 |
| Model class, features held fixed | +0.1167 ± 0.0023 |
| Interaction | +0.1397 ± 0.0024 |

The interaction is larger than either main effect, so the extra features are
worth little unless the model can exploit them non-linearly. Read this alongside
section 3, which shows most of the model-class effect is an artefact of how the
data was split.

## 3. Validation design

`scripts/04_validation_design.py`

This is the most transferable result here, and it is not specific to flood.

### Event concentration

| | |
|---|---|
| Nominal claims | 128,626 |
| Loss years | 49 |
| Effective independent observations | **3.55** |
| Design effect | ~36,000x |

Half the dataset comes from Hurricanes Katrina and Rita in 2005. Treating each
loss year as a cluster, `(Σn)² / Σn²` gives 3.55. The truth lies between perfect
within-event correlation and full independence, but the gap is large enough that
any standard error, p-value or confidence interval computed on an independence
assumption is materially overstated.

### Random against year-grouped cross-validation

| | Random 5-fold | Year-grouped |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | +0.0979 ± 0.0023 | +0.0564 ± 0.0820 |
| Elevation model | +0.1288 ± 0.0028 | +0.0884 ± 0.1006 |
| Gradient boosting | +0.3342 ± 0.0039 | +0.1212 ± 0.1323 |
| **Elevation lift** | **+0.0309 ± 0.0009** | **+0.0320 ± 0.0240** |
| **Boosting advantage** | **+0.2054 ± 0.0042** | **+0.0328 ± 0.0797** |

The elevation lift is unchanged, and slightly larger once event leakage is
removed. The boosting advantage falls by 84% and its interval includes zero.

Grouped folds are high variance: only five folds of heterogeneous years exist,
and removing a year removes its severity level entirely, which is why absolute
values can fall to or below zero. Read the lift comparison rather than the
levels.

### Leave one year out

The question a pricing actuary actually asks: if a model has never seen a
particular flood, does the elevation factor help it rank that flood?

Held out one year at a time across the 21 years with at least 500 claims, the
answer depends on weighting, and all views are reported rather than the best one.

| Basis | Elevation lift | Interval |
|---|---|---|
| Claim-weighted | +0.0233 | excludes zero |
| Five largest events | +0.0698 | positive in all five |
| All years weighted equally | +0.0087 | spans zero, positive in 12 of 21 |

A Gini computed on a five-hundred-claim year is mostly noise, and the baseline
behaves identically under the same treatment, so this is a property of the
estimator on thin data rather than a weakness of the variable. Intervals come
from a bootstrap that resamples whole years, since years are the independent
unit.

## 4. Surrounding terrain

`scripts/05_topographic_position.py`

Topographic Position Index, the cell elevation minus the mean of a surrounding
window excluding the centre, computed at 237 m, 394 m, 710 m, 2.6 km and 9.5 km.
Georeferencing is validated against the stored elevation: median absolute
difference 0.0000 m, 96.8% within 0.5 m.

| Added to the full terrain GLM | Random splits | Year-grouped |
|---|---|---|
| TPI at five scales | **+0.0460** | −0.0098 |
| Latitude and longitude | +0.0248 | −0.0351 |
| Coordinate node as a bare categorical | **+0.1301** | −0.0218 |

Under random splitting TPI appears to add more than the ERF itself. It does not
survive grouping, the boosted model gains nothing from it at all, and its
coefficients alternate sign across scales correlated at 0.93.

The cause is coordinate censoring. The claims occupy 572 distinct positions, so
TPI carries one value per location. A bare categorical label for the coordinate
node, holding no terrain information whatsoever, buys nearly three times as much
and fails the same way. The apparent gain is location identification.

An insurer holding true coordinates could compute TPI at a scale attributable to
the structure. This dataset cannot.

## 5. Other findings

**Elevated construction.** The NFIP elevated-building indicator is fully
populated, flagging 23.9% of the book. In the VE coastal zone the share is 91.3%
and the average paid loss is $27,971 against a portfolio $61,213, the lowest of
any zone. Elevated share by ERF band is U-shaped: 79.7% in the deepest
below-grade band, 10.7% at the near-BFE peak, 60.3% above six feet. Elevated
buildings cost about a third less in all eight bands. Adding the indicator to the
elevation model raises the Gini from 0.1252 to 0.1371, and its contribution grows
rather than shrinks under grouped validation, the only variable tested that does.

**Free public elevation.** A GLM using absolute DEM elevation with a zone
interaction, and no certified ERF, reaches 0.1013 against the baseline's 0.0954.
That recovers about 19% of the certified benefit under random splitting. Under
leave-one-year-out it is +0.009 to +0.011 on the years large enough to score
reliably, and cannot be distinguished from zero when all years are weighted
equally.

**Inflation.** Trending to constant dollars removes about 22% of the elevation
lift; controlling for loss year removes 79%. The gap is event identity rather
than price level. The deep below-grade pattern persists after trending, in fact
deepening slightly from 0.496 to 0.467.

## Data notes

- **The ERF is integer-valued.** Certified elevation differences are recorded in
  whole feet. 35% of claims sit at exactly 0 ft and a further 29% at +1 ft. Band
  intervals are right-closed; a left-closed cut disagrees on 56% of rows.
- **Losses are not capped** at any percentile. Paid building loss equals the raw
  FEMA amount for 100% of rows, maximum $692,480.
- **The post-FIRM indicator is not constant**, contrary to a common assumption:
  the split is 72,311 to 56,315. It is excluded from the boosted feature set
  because excluding it reproduces the published figure. Including it adds about
  0.016.
- **Coordinates are censored** to one decimal degree. 128,626 claims occupy 572
  distinct positions, and every DEM-derived feature inherits roughly 560 distinct
  values.
- **Construction year is missing** for about 12.3% of claims; the column median
  is imputed for the structure-age covariate.
- **Recent years are thin.** Claim counts fall to double or single digits after
  2021.

## Scope

Severity only. A complete pure-premium factor also needs a frequency component
estimated against an exposure base.

Louisiana only. The workflow and factor structure are state-agnostic; the
relativities are not, and should be re-estimated on a carrier's own geography.
